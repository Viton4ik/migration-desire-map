If you could move anywhere tomorrow, would you leave your country? We fly across 159 countries and mark where people want out, where they want in, and where they can't leave at all.

Gallup's polls show Canada and New Zealand pulling people in, while in Liberia (76%), Sierra Leone (75%) and Ghana (65%) most adults would leave. Two countries don't let their citizens go.

Coverage: 159 countries in 11 regions on every continent. Facts as of 4 November 2026.

How we measure it:
- Want to leave: Gallup World Poll, % of adults who would like to move permanently to another country (mostly 2023; older years are shown on screen).
- Want in vs want out: Gallup's Potential Net Migration Index (people who would move in minus people who would move out), 2023-2025.
- Dream destination: share of the world's would-be migrants who name the country (Gallup 2025).
- Where Gallup has no number, we use another national survey (dotted outline) or the UN's estimate of actual net migration, 2019-2023 (hatched). The UN figure counts real moves, not wishes.
- Can't leave: a documented exit ban (North Korea, Eritrea).
Different surveys ask different questions, so compare countries within one colour band, not as an exact ranking.

The final map: 16 magnets, 16 with more people moving in, 47 where most people stay, 52 where many want out, 26 where close to half or more want out, and 2 where people can't leave.
